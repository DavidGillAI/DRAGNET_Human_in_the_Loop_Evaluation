# Lab Summary

A human-in-the-loop evaluation was conducted in Argilla on 16 `gpt-4o-mini` claim-classification responses from the DRAGNET LangSmith dataset. Each justification was scored from 0 to 2 for evidence grounding, reasoning consistency, uncertainty handling and clarity, without showing the reviewer the hidden reference answer. Fourteen responses received the maximum score of 8, while the two claims classified incorrectly in LangSmith each scored 3, producing an overall average of 7.38 out of 8. Both failures involved the same reasoning error: the model treated insufficient evidence as proof that an unresolved claim was false. The results suggest that the model usually produces clear, evidence-based justifications but needs stronger instructions or safeguards for distinguishing `false` from `unknowable`. Argilla was deployed locally with Docker because the recommended Hugging Face Docker Space required a paid Pro subscription at the time of the lab. As this evaluation used one reviewer, the scores represent a single informed judgement rather than inter-annotator agreement.
